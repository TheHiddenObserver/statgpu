## Findings closed in this pass

1. **CRITICAL — public covariance non-finite input fail-closed contract.**
   `clustered_covariance`, `two_way_clustered_covariance`, `hac_covariance`, and
   `driscoll_kraay_covariance` reject non-finite `X`/residual arrays before
   signed/grouped reductions. This prevents NaN/Inf score rows from being silently
   converted into zero positive/negative contributions by the magnitude-tiered
   reducer. NumPy, maintained Torch CPU, and physical CuPy/Torch runner coverage
   are registered. The internal `ols_covariance` dispatcher remains internal and
   does not repeat the public-helper finite scan.

2. **HIGH — projection-created dynamic-range backend coverage.**
   Two-way demeaning refreshes its stability classification after the first entity
   projection. A dedicated fixture proves the transition from a safe raw vector to
   a risky post-projection vector in maintained Torch CPU and the physical
   CuPy/Torch validator.

3. **CRITICAL — order-dependent multi-tier two-way covariance reduction.**
   Maintained Torch 2.0 exposed a third-magnitude nonnested two-way covariance that
   passed in isolation but became `Inf` after earlier covariance tests. Signed
   component rows were still reduced inside BLAS before estimator-level
   Cameron-Gelbach-Miller cancellation. The final implementation keeps grouped row
   outer products separate only when a multi-tier case also fails the cross-group
   row-reduction safety certificate, decomposes group-debias factors into exact
   power-of-two terms, and uses compensated estimator-level summation. Gram-safe
   multi-tier cases remain vectorized and ordinary single-tier covariance is
   unchanged.

4. **MEDIUM — precision fallback scope/performance.**
   The row-level compensated fallback is limited to the pathological unsafe branch;
   Gram-safe multi-tier covariance retains vectorized BLAS/GPU component products.

5. **CRITICAL — fixed-effect recovery could reintroduce unstable group means.**
   One-way post-fit effect maps used raw scatter sums even though fitting had moved
   to the shared stable reducer. Two-way additive-effect recovery also did not
   refresh stability after an alternating projection, and centering every level
   residual before recovery could itself erase a low-order contribution. One-way
   maps now use the shared stable group mean on uncentered level residuals; two-way
   recovery refreshes/certifies stability as projections change dynamic range.
   NumPy/Torch public-prediction regressions and physical CuPy/Torch coverage lock
   the cancellation-tail behavior.

6. **CRITICAL — two-way effect normalization could overflow before cancellation.**
   The prior ALS loop repeatedly evaluated `time_effect * group_count`, which can
   overflow even when the observation-weighted normalization shift is finite. The
   common-shift normalization is now applied only after convergence and computes
   its shift with `stable_mean(time_effects[t_idx])`, avoiding both the product
   overflow and per-iteration stable-reduction overhead.

7. **CRITICAL — two-way common Gram scaling could silently erase a recoverable
   product.**
   A grouped component can remain nonzero after the common score scaling while its
   mathematically nonzero self/cross product underflows before inclusion-exclusion.
   A 2x2 nonnested fixture with scores `[-1e308, 1e-100, 1e308, 1e-100]` has a
   representable low-order CGM remainder. The implementation now detects this
   unsupported float64 product-range case and raises `FloatingPointError` rather
   than silently publishing zero. EN/CN covariance documentation records this
   explicit working-range boundary, and the physical CuPy/Torch runner exercises
   the same fail-closed contract.

8. **CRITICAL — centered fixed-effect maps could overflow although level
   predictions were finite.**
   A finite group mean and finite grand mean can have an unrepresentable centered
   difference. Fixed-effect maps are therefore stored directly on the level-
   residual scale; `_grand_mean` remains available for the established diagnostic
   contract but `PanelOLS.predict()` no longer needs to reconstruct fitted effects
   through a centered map. A 1-vs-10 `+/-1e308` fixture verifies finite exact
   known-label prediction on NumPy/Torch and is registered for physical CuPy/Torch.

9. **MEDIUM — obsolete known-label mask after the level-scale map change.**
   Once prediction stopped restoring the grand mean, its extra host-side `known`
   mask became dead O(n) work. The final prediction path removes that scan while
   retaining the same unseen-label and two-way incidence-component behavior.

10. **MEDIUM — stale physical-artifact documentation.**
    Existing P100 artifacts are explicitly historical reference evidence. They
    predate the latest shared-reduction, effect-recovery, product-range and map-
    representation fixes and cannot serve as exact-head physical acceptance.
