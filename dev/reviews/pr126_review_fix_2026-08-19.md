## Findings closed in the first fresh pass

1. **CRITICAL — public covariance non-finite input fail-closed contract.**
   `clustered_covariance`, `two_way_clustered_covariance`, `hac_covariance`, and
   `driscoll_kraay_covariance` reject non-finite `X`/residual arrays before
   signed/grouped reductions. NumPy, maintained Torch CPU, and physical CuPy/Torch
   runner coverage lock this API boundary.

2. **HIGH — projection-created dynamic-range backend coverage.**
   Two-way demeaning refreshes its stability classification after the first entity
   projection. Maintained Torch CPU and physical CuPy/Torch fixtures prove the
   safe-raw to risky-post-projection transition.

3. **CRITICAL — order-dependent multi-tier two-way covariance reduction.**
   A third-magnitude nonnested covariance could become `Inf` only after preceding
   Torch covariance tests because signed grouped components were reduced inside
   BLAS before Cameron-Gelbach-Miller cancellation. The unsafe multi-tier branch
   keeps grouped row products separate, decomposes group-debias factors into exact
   powers of two, and uses compensated estimator-level summation. Gram-safe
   multi-tier and ordinary single-tier paths remain vectorized.

4. **MEDIUM — precision fallback scope/performance.**
   Row-level compensated covariance accumulation is restricted to the pathological
   unsafe branch; Gram-safe multi-tier covariance stays on vectorized BLAS/GPU
   products.

5. **CRITICAL — fixed-effect recovery could reintroduce unstable group means.**
   Post-fit one-way maps had fallen back to raw scatter means and two-way recovery
   could miss projection-created cancellation risk. Effect recovery now reuses the
   shared stable group means on uncentered level residuals and certifies transformed
   residuals before accepting convergence.

6. **CRITICAL — two-way effect normalization could overflow before cancellation.**
   Repeated `time_effect * group_count` normalization could overflow even when the
   observation-weighted shift was finite. The normal-scale path defers the common
   shift until convergence and computes it with `stable_mean(time_effects[t_idx])`.

7. **CRITICAL — two-way common Gram scaling could erase a recoverable product.**
   A grouped component can survive common score scaling while a mathematically
   nonzero self/cross product underflows before inclusion-exclusion. Unsupported
   common-Gram product range now fails closed with `FloatingPointError` rather than
   silently publishing zero.

8. **CRITICAL — centered fixed-effect maps could overflow although predictions were
   finite.** Fixed-effect maps are stored directly on the level-residual scale so
   `PanelOLS.predict()` no longer constructs an unrepresentable centered effect.

9. **MEDIUM — obsolete fixed-effect prediction mask.** The dead host-side known-label
   scan was removed after level-scale FE maps eliminated grand-mean restoration.

10. **CRITICAL — pooling-F regressor centering could lose cancellation tails.**
    Diagnostic column means now reuse the shared magnitude-tiered stable mean.

11. **CRITICAL — sparse two-way incidence graphs could choose an out-of-range FE
    gauge.** Near the float64 boundary, additive entity/time effects use a
    range-minimizing common-shift gauge; ordinary-scale recovery keeps the
    historical weighted-zero gauge.

12. **CRITICAL — delayed covariance scale restoration had a range-unsafe
    multiplication association.** Covariance/projection/design factors are composed
    in mantissa/exponent form before bounded restoration.

13. **CRITICAL — stable means did not make physical `value - mean` centering safe.**
    Pooling F, constant-only classical F, parameter R-squared, and adjusted
    R-squared use a common dimensionless response working scale only when physical

14. **MEDIUM — stale physical-artifact documentation.** Existing P100 artifacts are

## Findings closed in the third independent pass

19. **CRITICAL — exact-constant panel solves could preserve the intercept but corrupt
    a smaller slope hidden beneath a huge common response level.**
    For `x=[1,1,-1,-1]` and `y=2**55 + [24,0,-8,-32]`, all inputs are exactly
    representable and the OLS slope is exactly `16`, while the previous SVD response
    projection could return about `14`. Full-rank solves with an exact leading
    constant now remove a range-safe midrange response anchor before projection and
    restore it only to the constant coefficient. `PooledOLS` and `BetweenOLS` no
    longer maintain a separate SVD implementation; their exact-constant helper
    validates/permutates the constant and delegates to the shared solver.

20. **CRITICAL — generic shared SVD could publish a finite but numerically unresolved
    non-constant coefficient even for a well-conditioned design.**
    A 4x2 design with condition number below two and exact real solution
    `[2**55, 16]` could produce a much smaller second coefficient and a material
    normal-equation residual. The shared solver now combines a conservative
    coordinate-resolution test with an independent least-squares stationarity
    certificate. Only a nonzero coordinate that is both at the roundoff scale of
    the absolute pseudoinverse projection and materially non-stationary fails
    closed with `FloatingPointError`; ordinary zeros, exact anchored intercepts,
    and well-resolved mixed-scale fits are not rejected. Public PanelOLS,
    PooledOLS, FirstDifferenceOLS, NumPy, and maintained Torch CPU regressions lock
    the boundary.

21. **CRITICAL — FirstDifferenceOLS legacy R-squared could overflow while physically
    centering a representable differenced fit.**
    Legacy R-squared now reuses the shared range-safe response-centering and
    residual-rescaling policy. A differenced response containing one `+1e308` and
    ten `-1e308` values retains the exact scale-invariant result `R²=1` with a
    representable `1e308` coefficient instead of overflowing `y-mean(y)`.

22. **MEDIUM — the Fama-MacBeth Gram precision certificate initially required a
    second factorization.**
    The final single-response fast path uses one augmented Gram solve to obtain both
    the candidate period coefficient and the inverse-Gram information needed for
    the coordinatewise right-hand-side roundoff bound. The safety certificate no
    longer doubles the Gram factorization cost.

23. **MEDIUM — a full-rank Fama-MacBeth coefficient-resolution failure was initially
    reported as rank deficiency.**
    Every Fama-MacBeth period design contains an explicit intercept, so genuine
    numerical rank cannot be zero. The existing packed rank transfer therefore
    uses zero exclusively as the shared fallback's coefficient-resolution sentinel.
    Fama-MacBeth now raises an explicit `FloatingPointError` identifying the
    retained period instead of a false rank-deficiency `ValueError`, with no new
    per-period GPU synchronization.

24. **MEDIUM — RandomEffects risky-response solves could bypass the new shared
    coefficient-resolution certificate.**
    The former `panel_lstsq_stable_response` duplicate SVD path has been removed;
    RandomEffects auxiliary and GLS solves now delegate to the same central panel
    least-squares policy as the other estimators.

25. **MEDIUM — third-pass three-backend/documentation coverage.**
    `dev/benchmarks/validate_panel_intercept_cancellation_gpu.py` now checks the
    shared constant-only cancellation, common-level slope, unresolved mixed
    coefficient fail-closed behavior, Fama-MacBeth resolution error and
    large-intercept Gram-certified path, FirstDifference extreme R-squared, and the
    previous RandomEffects fail-closed cases on both CuPy CUDA and Torch CUDA while
    recording exact SHA, clean-tree state, GPU/package versions, and executed
    backend provenance. Shared EN/CN panel docs plus Fama-MacBeth and
    FirstDifference pages document these limits.

## Findings closed in the fourth independent pass — 2026-08-20

26. **CRITICAL — a nonconstant Fama-MacBeth Gram RHS could round to exact zero and
    bypass the coefficient-resolution certificate.**
    The exact-intercept RHS had already been stabilized, but the remaining Gram
    coordinates were certified only when the candidate was nonzero. A condition-one
    period design with `x=[1,-1,1,-1]` and
    `y=[-3,2**55,2,-2**55]` has exact float-input sums
    `sum(y)=sum(x*y)=-1`, hence intercept=slope=`-1/4`, while ordinary BLAS can form
    `X'y=[-4,0]`. The stable intercept RHS repairs the first coordinate, but the
    nonconstant zero previously escaped the guard and could publish slope `0`.
    `_gram_resolution_risk_batched` now rejects zero as well as nonzero candidates
    that lie inside a nonzero RHS-roundoff error bound, while the separately stable
    exact intercept remains exempt.

27. **CRITICAL — the SVD fallback was not a valid rescue for an ambiguous exact-zero
    nonconstant RHS.**
    On the same condition-one fixture, even magnitude-tiered `U'y` summation can
    return a finite low-order slope far from `-1/4` because the singular vectors
    themselves are rounded. The unsafe Fama-MacBeth fallbacks therefore certify the
    original nonconstant RHS before publishing a coefficient: if the raw dot product
    is exactly zero while the absolute summand mass is nonzero, the backend-native
    rank sentinel is set to zero and the public estimator raises the established
    coefficient-resolution `FloatingPointError`. This is a fail-closed precision
    boundary, not a rank-deficiency claim.

28. **MEDIUM — stale solver-provenance regression after stable intercept RHS.**
    The maintained `[2**55,1,-2**55]` period-intercept fixture correctly retains
    intercept `1/3`, but the old test still required every period to take SVD
    fallback. Since the intercept Gram RHS is now itself magnitude-tiered, the
    correct optimized contract is `gram-certified` with zero fallbacks. Coefficient
    and slope oracles were unchanged; only the obsolete path assertion was updated.

29. **MEDIUM — fourth-pass three-backend/documentation coverage.**
    `dev/tests/test_panel_lstsq_resolution_precision.py` now locks both the stable
    intercept fast path and the condition-one ambiguous nonconstant RHS failure on
    NumPy and maintained Torch CPU. The focused physical runner
    `dev/benchmarks/validate_fama_macbeth_rhs_cancellation_gpu.py` requires the same
    behavior on CuPy CUDA and Torch CUDA while recording exact SHA, clean worktree,
    GPU/package versions, requested/executed backend, solver mode, and fallback
    provenance. English and Chinese Fama-MacBeth documentation explicitly separate
    common-level anchoring, exact-intercept cancellation, and ambiguous nonconstant
    zero-RHS fail-closed behavior.

## Rejected intermediate approaches

Strict statistical or regression oracles were not weakened to make implementation
failures pass. Rejected designs across the later passes include:

- treating only the response's raw dynamic-range classifier as sufficient for
  exact-constant solves; a huge common level can look numerically safe while hiding
  a smaller slope in the SVD projection;
- exact bitwise equality for the analytic slope `16`; the accepted oracle is eight
  float64 eps because SVD basis vectors are rounded, while the historical wrong
  result near `14` remains far outside that tolerance;
- blanket small-coordinate rejection, which incorrectly rejected already-supported
  Fama-MacBeth extreme-scale covariance/cancellation fixtures;
- a first resolution guard that added extra scalar synchronizations to
  `panel_lstsq`, violating the maintained performance contract;
- a global coefficient-size Gram certificate and a separate `solve(G, I)`, which
  either overrejected valid periods or added a second factorization;
- treating exact/anchored intercept zeros as unresolved coefficients;
- forcing every Fama-MacBeth Gram-RHS coordinate through magnitude-tiered scatter
  reduction on the ordinary fast path; that would expand the correctness fix into a
  broad CPU/GPU performance regression instead of isolating the rare unsupported
  zero-RHS case;
- treating a stable SVD projection as proof that an ambiguous-zero coefficient is
  correct; rounded singular vectors can still create a finite but wrong low-order
  coefficient even when the `U'y` summation itself is compensated;
- naive iterative refinement as proof of correctness: rounded residuals can become
  exactly zero even while the small coefficient remains wrong.

The earlier second-pass rejected designs remain documented in git history: an
observation-level stable-mean centering/restoration approach was backend-order
sensitive, and an elementwise near-zero RandomEffects comparison was too broad for
maintained full-rank near-collinear fits.
